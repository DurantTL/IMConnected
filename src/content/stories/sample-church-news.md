The first paragraph of a news story should answer five questions: **who** was involved, **what** happened, **when** and **where** it happened, and **why** it matters. A reader who stops after one paragraph should still know the story.

The next two paragraphs carry the most important details: how many people took part, what the outcome was, and what comes next.

Quotes from participants belong in the middle of the story. A good quote shares a feeling or perspective that a summary can’t.

Background — history of the ministry, earlier events, longer context — goes at the end.
